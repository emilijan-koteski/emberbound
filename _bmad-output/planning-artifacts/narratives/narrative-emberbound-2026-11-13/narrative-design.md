## Story Foundation

### Narrative Premise

Ten years ago Rowan Ashby, a Cindersworn fire-caster, burned the town of Harrowmere with fire from the Ember Below, and swore to pay with what he held dearest when the debt was called. On the night before his wedding the debt is called: Lord Maldrek takes Elin as the payment, and the bargain turns Rowan into a horned ember-beast. Rowan sets out to take her back by breaking the six seals. If he fails, Elin is paid into the Ember Below and Rowan ends as the wardens did: a spent debtor, bound to hold a seal for the Tithe.

**The goal under the goal.** Rowan's stated goal is to take Elin back, which means refusing to pay. The story's real question is what he owes and how he can pay it. He can't undo Harrowmere; he can only pay with what is his own. Version one turns that corner: it starts as a rescue and ends with Rowan standing in the town he burned.

### Core Themes

1. **Paying for what you did.** The central theme. Redemption here is paid, not granted. Nothing Rowan does makes Harrowmere right; each seal pays a little of what he owes.
   - *Question the story asks:* can you pay for something that can't be undone?
   - *Version one's answer:* you start by facing it.
2. **The cost lands on others.** A bargain's price spreads past the one who made it. Harrowmere paid for a lord's siege and Rowan's fire. Elin is being paid for a promise made before Rowan knew her. The wardens are debtors whose payments were taken.
3. **The body tells the truth.** The curse doesn't invent a monster; it shows what the Cindersworn already were. Becoming human is earned one seal at a time, and the player sees it rather than reads it. This is pillar 3 as a theme.

### Tone and Atmosphere

**Tone:**

| Spectrum | Emberbound |
| --- | --- |
| Dark ↔ lighthearted | Dark, not grim. Nothing gory. The horror is in what was done, not in what is shown. |
| Serious ↔ comedic | Serious in the story. The lightness lives in play: the bounce of a stomp, the snap of a hit. No jokes in story text. |
| Gritty ↔ fantastical | Fantastical, told like a folk tale or a cautionary fable |
| Intimate ↔ epic | Intimate: one man, one woman, one burned town. No kingdom to save. |
| Hopeful ↔ melancholic | Melancholic, with hope that grows a little at each seal |

**Atmosphere:**

- **Visual: a cold world, a warm hero.** The GDD's rule that warm light belongs only to Rowan, embers and lanterns carries the theme. Rowan's fire is both his crime and the only warmth on screen. Harrowmere is cold because its fire went out ten years ago.
- **Audio:** the bell is Elin's sound; the GDD already ties the bell effect to her. The music follows the GDD.
- **Pacing:** urgent in play, still in story moments. The intro cards, the seal break and the end of Harrowmere are short and quiet.
- **Text register:** plain, short lines, like a tale read aloud. One idea per line. No names the player hasn't been given, and no lore dumps.

**Emotional register:**

| Point | Feeling |
| --- | --- |
| Intro and Level 1 | Urgency and loss: she's gone, and you're a monster |
| Level 2 and the warden | Recognition: the warden is what Rowan could become. Beating him is a relief and a warning. |
| Level 3 and the closing card | Guilt and quiet, then a small, earned hope, and the surprise that Elin isn't waiting |
| Avoid | Triumph. The seal break is a release, not a victory. |

## Story Structure

### Structure Type

**Three acts across the full game. Version one is Act 1 and stands on its own.**

The six levels split 3 / 2 / 1 across the acts. Version one may be all most players ever see, so Act 1 carries its own small arc: denial, mirror, reckoning. The other structures don't fit. Branching needs choices, and the game has none. An episodic structure doesn't fit a body change that builds up. A hero's journey is about adventure, and this story is about a reckoning.

### Act Breakdown

**Act 1: The debt is called (version one, Levels 1 to 3).** Rowan thinks this is a rescue. It turns into a reckoning.

| Beat | Where | What happens | Delivered by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Setup | Intro | Harrowmere, the bargain, Elin, the wedding eve, Maldrek, the change | Intro cards (5 or fewer) |
| Denial | Level 1, Emberwood | Rowan runs through his own home forest in a body that isn't his. He learns it, and it works. | Level line, play, Rowan's form |
| Mirror | Level 2, the Drowned Mill, and the warden | The first warden is a debtor whose payment was taken long ago and who now holds a seal for the Tithe. Rowan sees what he will become. The first seal breaks: a horn breaks off, and his claw becomes a hand. | Level line, arena, the seal-break sequence |
| Reckoning | Level 3, Harrowmere | The way on runs through the town he burned. The hardest platforming in version one. No comment, just the ruins. | Level line, environment |
| Turn | Closing card | Rowan facing Harrowmere, his first payment. Elin acting on her own. | Closing card |
| Grace note | All-bells card | A memory of Elin the bell-maker | All-bells card |

Act 1 ends on the full game's first turning point: Rowan stops trying to take Elin back and starts trying to pay what he owes.

**Act 2: The way down (Levels 4 and 5, after version one).** A sketch only.

- Bellspire Pass and the Tithe Road.
- Rowan pays rather than takes. Elin's own thread runs alongside his, and the two meet.
- The low point comes before the Hollow Court.
- The link between Bellspire and Elin the bell-maker is worth exploring in the world step.

**Act 3: The Hollow Court (Level 6, after version one).** Maldrek, then the pact. The ending, with Elin taking part, is chosen later in this workflow, before Epic 5 writes the closing card.

**Ending decision (2026-11-13).** The full game builds toward the GDD's candidates 1 and 2 combined: Elin learns what Rowan did at Harrowmere, rings the bell that breaks the last seal by her own choice, and Rowan gives up the fire. Whether she stays with him afterwards is left as her choice.

**Open for after version one:** giving up the fire at the climax removes Rowan's only attack, so the final fight needs another way to play.

**Act 1, Setup: the debt is called (version one).** Beats 1 to 12.

- Intro: beats 1 to 4.

**Act 2, Confrontation: the way down (after version one).** Beats 13 to 15. Elin's thread and Rowan's run in parallel. The midpoint reveal is told from her side. The act ends at the low point.

**Act 3, Resolution: the Hollow Court (after version one).** Beats 16 to 18. The ending: Elin learns the truth, rings the bell by her own choice, and Rowan gives up the fire.

## Pacing and Flow

### Narrative Tempo

The game runs fast and the story waits at the doors. In play the story never stops the action. It lives in Rowan's body and in the places he passes. Story text and sequences sit only at level boundaries and at the seal break:

- the intro: 60 s at most, skippable
- one line before each level
- the seal break: 15 s at most, skippable on replays
- the closing card
- the all-bells card

That is roughly 2 minutes of story moments in a 20 to 30 minute run. This is a guess until the cards and the sequence are timed.

### Tension Curve

There are two curves in version one, and their peaks are deliberately apart:

| Moment | Play intensity | Story weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intro | None | High: loss and the change |
| Level 1, Emberwood | Low, rising to medium | Low: learning the body |
| Level 2, the Drowned Mill | Medium | Rising: someone else's bargain |
| The warden | **Peak** | High: the mirror |
| The seal break | Release | High: the first change |
| Level 3, Harrowmere | Medium-high, the hardest platforming | **Peak:** guilt |
| Closing card | None | The turn: hope, and Elin acting |

The warden is the loudest moment and Harrowmere is the heaviest. The quiet after the seal break leads into Harrowmere, so the player arrives already changed and walks into the consequence.

### Story Density

| Section | Density | Carried by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intro | Heavy | Up to 5 illustrated cards |
| Level 1 | Light | The level line and Rowan's form. Home turned strange. |
| Level 2 | Medium | The level line and the drowned mill's environment. The warden's story is told by his arena and his body. |
| Seal break | Heavy, without words | An in-engine sequence |
| Level 3 | Heavy, without words | The level line and the ruins. No text inside the level. |
| Closing and all-bells cards | Heavy | One card each |

**Required:** the intro (skippable), the level lines, the seal break (skippable on replays), the closing card.
**Optional:** the all-bells card (all 9 bells), and environmental details along detours and at bell spots.

### Key Moments

**Highest tension:** version one, the Warden of the First Seal at 15 HP, when phase 2 starts. Full game: Maldrek in the Hollow Court.
**Emotional climax:** version one, facing Harrowmere (beat 10). Full game: Elin rings the bell knowing what Rowan did (beat 17).
**Resolution beat:** version one, the closing card (beat 11). Full game: After (beat 18).

## Characters

### Protagonist(s)

#### Rowan Ashby

**Description:** A former Cindersworn fire-caster in his mid-thirties, cursed into a horned ember-beast. About 3 heads tall. Charcoal skin with glowing seams, two bone horns sweeping back, a furnace glow in his chest, ember-bright eyes. A torn red woodsman's scarf is his human trace. Determined, not monstrous.

**Background:** Ten years ago he was a hired fire-caster. On a lord's order he burned the granary town of Harrowmere to end a siege, with fire from a bargain with the Ember Below. He left that life, settled at the edge of Emberwood as a woodsman, and told no one, including Elin, what he had been.

**Motivation:**

- *On the surface:* take Elin back.
- *Underneath:* stay the man Elin knows, which means never having been the man who burned Harrowmere.
- *What the story asks of him:* pay what he owes.

**Strengths:** He keeps going. He knows the forest. He has the fire, which is also the problem.

**Flaws:**

- He hides what he did.
- When cornered he reaches for the fire. That reflex is what cursed him.
- His instinct is to take back rather than to pay.

**Conflicts:**

- Internal: he wants to be the man Elin loves, but that man was built on a silence. Every step toward her goes through what he hid.
- External: Maldrek and the wardens, a body that isn't his, and a road that runs through Harrowmere.

#### Elin (deuteragonist)

**Description:** A bell-maker, about Rowan's age. Practical, patient and stubborn, with a craftswoman's hands. Her craft needs fire too. She uses it to make things that call people together; Rowan used it to destroy.

**Background:** The bell-maker of the village at the edge of Emberwood, and Rowan's bride-to-be. She has no tie to Harrowmere. She was the payment for a promise made before Rowan knew her, and she was never asked.

**Motivation:** In version one, to get herself free, not to wait. In the full game, to decide for herself what she owes Rowan once she knows what he did.

**Strengths:** Her craft. Patience. She makes things that last and can be heard from far away.

**Flaws:** Pride in her own judgment. She chose Rowan, and the truth means she chose without knowing.

**Conflicts:**

- Internal (from the midpoint): the man she loves against what she now knows he did.
- External: captivity in the Hollow Court, and the Tithe, which treats her as a payment.

---

### Antagonist(s)

#### Lord Maldrek, the Tithe-Collector

**Description:** The Tithe's enforcer. Cold, procedural and polite: a creditor, not a ranter. He carries no warm light; on screen he belongs with the cold world, never with Rowan's fire.

**Background:** He was a debtor once. When his own debt was called, he made a second bargain: he would collect for the Ember Below, and as long as he collects, his own debt waits. He has been putting off his payment longer than anyone.

**Goals:** Keep collecting, so he never pays. Elin is one more payment. Broken seals threaten the arrangement that keeps his own debt waiting.

**Methods:**

- He arrives at the moment a debt is called and takes what was promised.
- He binds spent debtors as wardens to hold the seals.
- He doesn't fight until the end. The Tithe and its wardens do the work.

**Relationship to Protagonist:** Rowan's creditor and his foil. At the start Rowan wants what Maldrek has: to take instead of pay. Rowan's arc carries him away from Maldrek.

**Sympathetic Elements:** Fear. He is the one person who has never paid, and every year makes the bill larger. This is not shown in version one. What he owes, and to whom, is open for Act 3.

#### The Warden of the First Seal: the Drowned Miller

*An opponent, not a villain.* Internal given name: Tobin Weir, a placeholder that needs an originality check before it appears on screen.

**Description (for Epic 4 art):**

- A hunched miller fused with the great waterwheel of his mill. The wheel rises from his back like a shell, its paddles split and dripping.
- Iron cog teeth run along his back and shoulders. These are the spikes that make him unstompable under the thorn rule.
- River water pours from him all the time. Pale, weed-hung, cold. No warm light anywhere on him.
- His face is tired, not cruel.
- His body fits the GDD's attacks:
  - *Wheel wash:* the wheel spins up and drives a wave along the floor.
  - *Millstone fall:* the mill's stones and beams come down from the rafters.
  - *Wheel roll:* he curls into his own wheel and rolls across the arena.

**Background:** His mill ground Harrowmere's grain. When Rowan burned the town, the grain stopped and the mill stood idle, and he was ruined. He loved the mill above everything, so he bargained with the Ember Below: *let my wheel never stop.* The river rose to keep it turning and drowned the valley into marsh. He paid with what he held dearest, the mill itself, and it took him in. Maldrek bound him to hold the first seal.

**Goals:** None of his own now. He turns, and he holds the seal.

**Relationship to Protagonist:**

- Rowan's fire caused his ruin, so the mirror is also Rowan's victim.
- He is what Rowan becomes if he fails: a debtor whose payment was taken, bound to the Tithe.
- Version one never states the link. The level shows it for players who look (detailed in the world step).

**Sympathetic Elements:** All of him. He only wanted to keep working.

**Fate:** Released. When the seal breaks, the wheel stops turning for the first time in ten years. He goes still and sinks into calm water, at rest. Breaking the seal frees a debtor and kills no one. It is the first thing Rowan's fight gives back to someone else.

### Supporting Characters

#### The Ember Below

**Role:** The power beneath the world. Not a character.
**Personality:** None shown. It grants what is asked, to the letter, and takes what is dearest.
**Function:** The source of every bargain. It is never given a face or a voice, which keeps the folk-tale register and keeps clear of D&D's "patron" idea.
**Key Moments:** The bargain (intro), the change (intro), the seal break (felt, not seen).

#### The lord who ordered the burning

**Role:** Offscreen, unnamed.
**Personality:** Not shown.
**Function:** He shares the blame, but the story never lets Rowan hide behind him. Rowan carried the fire. Whether he returns as a later warden is open for after version one.
**Key Moments:** Intro card 1, as an order and not a face.

#### The people of Harrowmere

**Role:** Present by their absence.
**Personality:** Shown only through what they left behind.
**Function:** The cost of Rowan's act, made physical in Level 3 (detailed in the world step).
**Key Moments:** Level 3 and the closing card.

## Character Arcs

### Rowan Arc (positive; the transformation runs backwards, from beast to human)

**Starting State:** A man who hides what he did and, when cornered, reaches for the fire. He believes he can take Elin back.

**Transformation Moments:**

1. The change: his act becomes visible on his body.
2. The Drowned Miller: he fights a debtor his own fire ruined, and frees him.
3. Harrowmere: he faces what he did. This is his first payment and version one's turn.
4. Elin learns the truth from someone else. His secret is out, and not by his choice.
5. He gives up the fire.

**Ending State:** Human, without the fire, with nothing hidden. The debt is owned, not erased. Whether Elin stays with him is not his to decide.

**Lessons Learned:** You can't take back what you owe. You can only pay it, with what is yours.

### Elin Arc (her character holds; she moves from being bargained over to deciding)

**Starting State:** A bell-maker about to marry, paid away without being asked.

**Transformation Moments:**

1. Captivity, and she starts making a bell (version one's closing card).
2. She learns what Rowan did (the midpoint).
3. She rings the bell knowing everything (the climax).

**Ending State:** Free by her own hand. Her choice about Rowan is hers.

**Lessons Learned:** A bargain made over her head doesn't bind her.

### Maldrek Arc (flat and negative: he never changes, and the bill arrives)

**Starting State:** The collector who has put off his own payment longer than anyone.

**Transformation Moments:** Seals break, and his arrangement starts to fail.

**Ending State:** Candidate: when the last seal breaks, his own debt is finally called. Act 3 detail, after version one.

**Lessons Learned:** None. He is the man who never learns to pay.

### The Drowned Miller Arc (short: bound, then released)

**Starting State:** Bound to a wheel that never stops.

**Transformation Moments:** The seal breaks.

**Ending State:** Still and at rest.

**Lessons Learned:** None stated. His release shows the player what breaking a seal means.

## World Building

### World Overview

**Setting:** An unnamed rural land of forests, river valleys, farm towns and mountains. Lords besiege each other's towns. Under it all lies the Ember Below. Technology is at folk-tale level: mills, forges, bell foundries, carts and lanterns. No gunpowder.

**World Type:** Dark fantasy told as a folk tale. The rules are simple, old and a little cruel, as in a fable.

**World Rules: the Tithe.** These rules shape the art, the levels and the few lines of text. None is explained on screen.

1. **Anyone can bargain with the Ember Below, and every bargain has the same price:** *I will pay with what I hold dearest when the debt is called.*
2. **The Ember Below grants exactly what is asked, word for word.** The miller asked that his wheel never stop; the river rose and drowned the valley to keep it turning.
3. **The Tithe waits.** A debt is called only when the debtor holds something worth taking. Rowan held nothing dear for ten years, until Elin.
4. **Debtors end as what they asked for.** Rowan reached for the fire once more and became a creature of it. The miller paid with his mill and became part of it.
5. **The land pays too.** Where a bargain is made or paid, the land around it twists. Creatures grow thorns, spit seeds and turn wrong. Each place twists in its own colours, which is why each level's enemies are recoloured.
6. **Six seals hold the Tithe together.** Each is held by a warden, and every bargain hangs on them. Breaking a seal loosens the Tithe's hold on all its debtors: the warden who held it is released, and Rowan's curse loosens.
7. **The Tithe binds only debtors.** It has no hold on anyone who never bargained. *Not stated in version one. It pays off at the climax, when Elin rings the bell.*
8. **Embers.** Sparks of the Ember Below drift up wherever its fire has touched the land. Rowan's furnace draws them in, and they mend him.

**Atmosphere:** A cold world with a warm hero. The only warm light belongs to Rowan's fire, the embers, and lanterns hung by people. See Tone and Atmosphere.

**Unique Elements:**

- The debt waits until you love something.
- The enemies are the land paying for bargains.
- The collectables are a trail the captive left herself.

### Factions and Organizations

#### The Tithe

**Purpose:** To collect what is owed for every bargain with the Ember Below.
**Leadership:** Lord Maldrek, the Tithe-Collector.
**Structure:** Six wardens, each a spent debtor bound to hold one seal.
**Goals:** Every debt paid, except the Collector's own.
**Methods:** Wait until a debtor holds something dear. Take it. Bind the spent debtors as wardens.
**Relationships:** The Cindersworn are among its surest debtors. Lords pay them for fire, and the Tithe collects later.
**Story Role:** The opposition. Each warden is a boss, and each is someone the Tithe used up.

#### The Cindersworn

**Purpose:** Fire-casters for hire, sold to lords for sieges.
**Leadership:** None. It is a trade, not an order.
**Goals:** The work and the pay.
**Methods:** Fire from a bargain with the Ember Below. Every Cindersworn has made one.
**Relationships:** Feared by common folk, hired by lords, all in debt to the Tithe.
**Story Role:** Rowan's past. Other Cindersworn may appear after version one, possibly as wardens. That is open.

### Key Locations

The road: Maldrek takes Elin down the old road the Tithe walks, through places where bargains were paid. Rowan follows the same road, guided by her bells. Each level is a place a bargain marked. The story never fixes how many bells there are; a six-level game holds more than version one's nine.

#### The village at the edge of Emberwood (intro only)

**Description:** Rowan and Elin's home, with Elin's bell foundry.
**Narrative Significance:** Where the debt is called. The last whole, warm place in the story.
**Atmosphere:** Warm and human, seen only in the intro cards.
**Key Events:** The wedding eve, Maldrek's arrival, the change (beats 3 and 4).
**Inhabitants:** Villagers, Elin and Rowan.

#### Emberwood (Level 1)

**Description:** Rowan's home forest at dusk: old trees, mossy rock, wooden ledges. Lanterns hang along the path where the wedding in the wood was to be held.
**Narrative Significance:**

- Home made strange.
- The wood twisted the night Rowan was claimed. His own bargain is why it is hostile now: the first sign that his costs land on others.
- Elin's first bells.

**Atmosphere:** Dusk, warm lanterns hung for a celebration, and something wrong creeping into a familiar place.
**Key Events:** A body that isn't his (beat 5).
**Inhabitants:** Twisted forest creatures.

#### The Drowned Mill (Level 2)

**Description:**

- A river valley drowned into marsh: black water, fog, drowned fields and fence lines.
- Smaller waterwheels turn everywhere as the bargain spreads.
- The great wheel can be heard throughout the level, louder as Rowan nears the arena.
- Sacks of grain marked with Harrowmere's sign rot in the mill.

**Narrative Significance:** Someone else's bargain, and the first warden, both mirror and victim. The first seal breaks here.

## Dialogue Framework

Version one has no dialogue during play (GDD). This section defines the voice of the story text and how characters are heard without speech.

### Dialogue Style

**Overall Voice:** A folk-tale narrator: unseen, in the third person and the past tense, like a tale read aloud. Plain words and short sentences, one idea per line. The narrator says what happened and leaves the feeling to the player. The text frames the story; the body and the places carry it (pillar 3).

**Style Elements:**

- Formality: plain, with a light folk register. Not archaic: no "thee" or "thou", no mock-medieval spelling.
- Period: timeless. No modern idioms or slang.
- Verbosity: very concise. One line per card, about 80 characters at most. That limit is a guess, checked on screen in Epic 5.
- Humor: none in the story text.
- Profanity: none.

**Rules of the voice:**

1. **Concrete over abstract.** "He burned the town", never "he did a terrible thing".
2. **Name events, not emotions.** Feelings are rarely named; events carry them.
3. **Use the GDD's on-screen terms exactly:** Cindersworn, the Tithe, the Ember Below, Lord Maldrek the Tithe-Collector, Emberfire, the wardens of the six seals. No other invented terms in version one's text, and no name the player hasn't been given.
4. **The narrator neither judges Rowan nor forgives him,** and never hints that Elin will learn the truth.
5. **Past tense throughout,** in the intro, the level lines and the cards.
6. **The keywords are the oath's words:** *pay, dearest, debt, called.* Reuse them on purpose and sparingly, so later lines echo the oath.

**Quoted Speech:** Exactly two quoted lines in version one, both in the intro:

1. **The bargain:** Rowan's old oath, word for word: *"I will pay with what I hold dearest when the debt is called."*
2. **Maldrek calls the debt** on the wedding-eve card.
   - About 8 words at most.
   - It echoes the oath's words, so the promise and its collection echo each other.
   - Cold, procedural and polite: a creditor, not a threat. No gloating.
   - The exact wording is written in the delivery step.

No one else is quoted in version one.

**Character Voice Distinctions:** Characters are heard through signature sounds, not speech.

| Character | Voice in version one |
| --- | --- |
| Narrator | The story text, as above |
| Rowan | The ember spit (the GDD's signature sound), his furnace breath, and his hurt and death sounds. No words and no grunted speech. *Audio idea, not committed:* after the seal break his breath sounds slightly more human. |
| Elin | The bell. Every bell pickup is her voice. She is never quoted in version one. *Candidate for the full game:* her first quoted line comes at the climax. |
| Lord Maldrek | His one quoted line in the intro. *Audio idea:* sound drops away around him. |
| The Drowned Miller | The wheel: creak and rush, louder through Level 2, plus his three attack warnings (GDD). The wheel stopping is his last word. |
| The Ember Below | No voice, ever. |

## Environmental Storytelling

Version one tells most of its story here. The text sets the frame; the places, the body and the sound carry the rest (pillar 3).

**Placement principle:** story details sit where players pause: checkpoints, calm spots, bell detours and the start of each section. Never mid-jump, and never in the play layer where they would compete with hazards or pickups (the GDD's readability rules).

### Visual Storytelling

**Set Dressing:** Each level's environment kit carries a few story props, placed in the middle-distance and foreground layers, never on the play layer.

**Environmental Details:**

*Emberwood (Level 1): home turned strange*

- **Start:** the forest edge below the village. In the far layer, the cold chimney of Elin's bell foundry.
- **Lanterns** hang along the path, lit for tomorrow's wedding in the wood.
- **Twisted creatures** where the wood turned the night Rowan was claimed.
- **Control signs** are carved woodsman's waymarkers, with the icon burned into the wood.
- **Elin's bells**, the first of her trail, from section 2 on (GDD).
- **Exit:** the last calm stretch is the wedding glade, set up and empty: benches, lanterns and a garland arch. The exit runs under the arch. Rowan leaves home through his own wedding.

*The Drowned Mill (Level 2): someone else's bargain*

- **Start:** the valley rim, looking down on the drowned valley in fog.
- **The drowning:** fence lines and fields under black water. Grain carts half-sunk on the drowned road, which still points toward Harrowmere.
- **The spreading wheels:** small waterwheels on posts turn everywhere. The bargain spreads. These are the GDD's waterwheel platforms, so the mechanic has a story reason.
- **At the warden's door checkpoint:** rotting grain sacks marked with Harrowmere's sign, stacked by the millhouse door. This is the hidden link between the Miller's ruin and Rowan's fire. It is never stated, and it sits where the player pauses before the fight.
- **The arena:** inside the millhouse, the Miller fused with the great wheel.
- **The seal:** the hub of the great wheel, marked with the Tithe's sign. When it shatters, the wheel stops.

*Harrowmere (Level 3): the town he burned*

- **Start: the old siege line.** On a rise outside the walls: a rotted siege camp, a faded banner of the unnamed lord, and a scorched circle where the fire was cast. The player starts where Rowan stood ten years ago and walks the way the fire went.
- **The gate:** burned, with Harrowmere's mark, the same sign as on the mill's sacks.
- **The town:**
  - Charred walls, fallen beams and collapsed granaries.
  - Household things left where they were: a table set under ash, a door left open, a cart still loaded.
  - No bodies and no child imagery. Dark, not grim.
- **The climb:** ledge-grab routes through the ruins. The hand the seal gave back is what gets him through the town he burned.
- **Bells:** Elin passed through here too, not knowing whose ruin it was. Hints use ember trails and gaps; no lanterns, because no one is left to light them.
- **Exit: the fallen fire bell.** After the last hard section, a short calm stretch with no enemies leads to the square. Harrowmere's fire bell lies cracked where its tower fell. That is where Rowan stops. The last in-engine frame and the closing card should match.

*Rowan (all levels)*

- **Stage 1, Levels 1 and 2:** the full beast.
- **Stage 2, Level 3:** the broken horn, the human right hand, and a dimmer chest glow. Replays follow the GDD's replay-form rule.
- **The red scarf** is his human trace in every stage.

**Visual Symbolism:**

| Symbol | Means | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bells | Elin: calling people home, and her own choices | The collectables, the fallen fire bell, the closing card; Bellspire later |
| Lanterns | Warmth made by people's hands | Emberwood only (the wedding). Their absence in Levels 2 and 3 matters. |
| Wheels | A debt that never stops turning | The Drowned Mill, the waterwheel platforms, the warden. A wheel stopping means release. |
| Harrowmere's mark | The consequence that follows Rowan | The sacks at the mill and the gate of Harrowmere. Placeholder design: a sheaf of grain in a ring. Check it for originality. |
| The Tithe's sign | The Tithe's hold | The seal (the wheel's hub); later wardens |
| Horns | The curse | Rowan. One breaks per seal. |
| The red scarf | The man he still is | Rowan, in every stage |

**Color and Lighting:** This is the narrative layer. It holds whichever art style Epic 0 locks.

- **Warmth fades across version one.**
  - Emberwood has the most warm light: the wedding lanterns, home.
  - The Drowned Mill has almost none: cold fog and black water.
  - Harrowmere has none but Rowan and the embers. The town that burned is the coldest place in the game.
- **Rowan's own glow dims at the seal break** (GDD), so in Harrowmere the warmest thing on screen is a dimmer Rowan.
- **Pits and deep water read as dark** (GDD readability). Story lighting never overrides it.

## Gameplay Integration

### Narrative-Gameplay Connection

**Integration Approach:** The story is carried by what the player does and sees. Text frames the levels; the body, the abilities and the places carry the meaning (pillar 3). The one story-driven mechanic in version one is ledge grab, given back by the first seal.

**Mechanic-Theme Alignment:**

| Mechanic | Theme it carries |
| --- | --- |
| Ember spit, Rowan's only attack | The fire is both his weapon and his crime. The curse is useful, which is the trap (theme 1). |
| Embers heal | He survives by drawing in the Ember Below's sparks: he leans on the thing that cursed him. Resolved in the full game, when he gives the fire up. |
| The seal break and ledge grab | Theme 3: the body tells the truth. The human hand is the ability. |
| Replay-form rule (GDD) | The ability always matches the body the player sees. |
| Ledge grab in Harrowmere | He climbs through the town he burned with the hand the seal gave back. |
| Bells | Collecting them means following Elin, and the pickup plays her motif. |
| Linear levels (GDD) | There is no route around Harrowmere. The player can't avoid the consequence, and neither can Rowan. |
| Harrowmere as the hardest platforming | The difficulty peak and the moral reckoning land in the same place. |
| The warden: unstompable, 30 HP | The longest fight is against a victim of Rowan's fire. It is won, and it ends in release, not death. |

**Story-Gameplay Balance:** About 2 minutes of story moments in a 20 to 30 minute run, a guess until timed. Story text and sequences sit only between levels, at the seal break and at the held beat.

**Ludonarrative Considerations:**

- **Accepted dissonance: killing with the crime's fire.** Enemy deaths use the GDD's plain arcade burst. Players don't read arcade kills as moral acts. The theme is carried by the warden (released, not killed), Rowan's body and Harrowmere. The tension between the fire as weapon and the fire as crime is real, and it resolves in the full game when Rowan gives up the fire.
- **Respawns** have an in-world reason that is never shown: the Tithe doesn't let a debtor go before the debt is paid. This fits the stakes, where failure means becoming a warden, not dying.
- **Weapon pickups** (triple shot, piercing shot) are an arcade convention with no story. Visually they belong to the fire: warm, part of Rowan's palette.
- **Clear time** excludes story moments (GDD), so the held beat and the cards never cost a speedrunner.
- **For the full game:** giving up the fire removes the only attack, so the payment is felt in the player's hands. That fight's design is open (see Story Beats).

### Localization

**Approach:** English only (GDD).
**Future consideration:**

- Story text lives in one data module (see the Dialogue Framework).
- Card text is rendered on top of the silhouette art and never baked into the image. Both together make localization possible later at no cost now.
- If the game is ever localized:
  - Allow about 30% text growth. Card text boxes should take about 105 characters, or wrap to two lines.
  - Translate the oath first. Its keywords (*pay, dearest, debt, called*) are echoed in later lines, and the echoes have to survive translation.
  - Cultural adaptation needs are low: bells, weddings and debts are widely understood.

---

### Voice Acting

**Approach:** Text only, with non-verbal character sounds: Rowan's hurt and death sounds (GDD) and the furnace-breath idea from the Dialogue Framework.

**Rationale:**

- No dialogue in play, and the GDD's asset list has no voice-over.
- The narrator works as text.
- Voice-over would add a recording dependency and depends on the audio tools, which are still open.
- *Candidate for a later Steam build, not planned:* narrator voice-over for the intro only.

## Appendix: Character Relationships

### Relationship Map

```text
                           THE EMBER BELOW
             source of every bargain; never seen or heard
       its debtors: Lord Maldrek, Rowan Ashby, the Drowned Miller
                                  :
  LORD MALDREK ------ calls the debt, takes her ------> ELIN
  Tithe-Collector                                       deuteragonist
       |                                                 ^       |
       | binds to the first seal          loves her,     |       | hides her bells
       v                                  hid his past   |       v as a trail
  THE DROWNED MILLER                                    ROWAN ASHBY
  warden of the first seal <---- fights, then frees --- protagonist
       ^                                                 |       ^
       | ruined when the town burned                     |       | paid him
       | (never stated)                         burned   |       | to burn it
       |                                                 v       |
       +-------------------------------------------- HARROWMERE  THE UNNAMED LORD
```

### Relationship Key

- Rowan → Elin: loves her; hid his past from her; follows her bells.
- Elin → Rowan: loves him; doesn't know what he did until the midpoint; leaves her bells so he can follow; at the climax, chooses for herself.
- Lord Maldrek → Rowan: his creditor; calls the debt. His foil: the debtor who never paid.
- Lord Maldrek → Elin: takes her as payment; holds her in the Hollow Court.
- Lord Maldrek → the Drowned Miller: bound him to hold the first seal.
- Rowan → the Drowned Miller: his fire ruined him, though no one says so. Rowan fights him, and breaking the seal releases him. The Miller is what Rowan becomes if he fails.
- Rowan → Harrowmere: burned it. Facing it is his first payment.
- The unnamed lord → Rowan: paid him to burn Harrowmere. He shares the blame, but the story never lets Rowan hide behind him.
- The Ember Below → Maldrek, Rowan and the Miller: the source of each one's bargain.
- Elin → the Tithe: she never bargained, so it has no hold on her. This pays off at the climax.
