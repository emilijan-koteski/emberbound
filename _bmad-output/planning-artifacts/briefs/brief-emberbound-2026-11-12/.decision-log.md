# Decision log: Emberbound game brief

Canonical record of decisions, changes and overrides made while writing this brief. Newest entries at the bottom.

## 2026-11-12

- **Intent: create.** Started with `gds-create-game-brief` from `docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`, the pre-BMad planning brief. No earlier BMad brief existed.
- **Source of truth.** Once this brief and the GDD exist, they win over `docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md` on conflicts (rule recorded in `CLAUDE.md`).
- **Stack settled by the developer before this session:** three.js + TypeScript + Vite, Tripo for meshes, Blender through MCP for cleanup, rigging and animation, Claude models through Claude Code and BMad for development. Still open: image model, audio tools, level editor (LDtk or Tiled).
- **Stakes, from the source brief:** goals in order are learn game development, enjoy the process, finish something small and polished, possibly sell it cheaply. The brief is sized for a solo passion and learning project, not a publisher pitch.
- **Developer's first answer on the hook:** "simple to understand, and the story pulls the player toward the resolution." "Simple to understand" is a pillar candidate. Challenged: the source brief delivers story as one line per level with no dialogue during play, which may be too thin to be the main hook. Pending.
- **Developer's first answer on audience:** "everyone: the older generation who grew up on platformers, and the new generation that loves good platformers." Challenged as too broad. These are two segments with conflicting needs (e.g. the source brief's "one setting, tuned generous" difficulty). Asked which is primary. Pending.
- **Primary audience decided:** players who grew up on arcade and console platformers (roughly 35 to 55). Younger fans of modern platformers are secondary; when the two conflict, the primary group wins. This supports the source brief's single, generous difficulty.
- **Working mode: fast path.** Remaining gaps are batched into one question set; the draft will mark inferences as `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **Research returned** (digest in `addendum.md`). Two findings were re-checked against their sources:
  - "Emberbound" is taken on Steam by an upcoming visual novel (app 4634770). At least five other games use the name. The title stays a working title; renaming is on the open list.
  - itch.io takes only donations for browser-playable games; charging requires a downloadable build. This changes the earlier guess on distribution (question 4).
- **Price signal from comparables:** the Toki remake at $19.99 was criticised as too short; The Dark Queen of Mortholme, 20 to 40 minutes at €4.99, reviewed well. Steam refunds any game played under 2 hours.
- **Batch answers: "guesses fine".** Accepted as decisions:
  1. **Retention:** how the game feels holds the first 2 minutes (movement, jump, ember spit, the first enemy). The story carries the player to the end, mostly through what they see: Rowan's body changing, the places, each seal breaking. Text stays minimal.
  2. **Time:** about 8 to 10 hours a week, no fixed deadline.
  3. **MVP:** Level 1 playable from start to finish with final art (milestone M3). It proves the idea if a new player finishes it without help and wants Level 2. Version one (3 levels, 1 warden) is the first release, not the proof.
  4. **Distribution:** the whole of version one is free to play in the desktop browser on itch.io, with donations; keyboard and gamepad. A paid desktop build on Steam comes later, only if the free version finds players. This supersedes the earlier "free demo, paid on itch.io" guess, which itch.io's rules rule out for browser games.
  5. **Price, if paid:** $5 or less.
  6. **Audio direction:** one short, hummable tune per level that recalls the arcade era but uses modern instruments; punchy effects, with the ember spit as the signature sound.
  - Optional question 6 (what the game must never be) was skipped.
- **Inconsistency found in the source brief, left for the GDD:** the level table breaks the first seal in Level 2, but the milestones build the first warden (M4) before Levels 2 and 3 (M5). Where version one's single boss sits is open.
- **First full draft written** with `[ASSUMPTION]` tags on: core fantasy, vision statement, core loop, pillars, take/leave per comparable, differentiators, and the main risk mitigations.
- **Assumptions confirmed: "assumptions fine".** All eight `[ASSUMPTION]` items in the draft are now decisions: core fantasy, vision statement, core loop, the three pillars, leaving form-switching from The Dragon's Trap, the differentiators, the hand-built hero fallback, and the Level 1 playtest check on whether players understand Rowan. Tags removed from `brief.md`.
- **Polish passes.** The configured skills `bmad-editorial-review-structure` and `bmad-editorial-review-prose` are not installed. The structure and prose lenses inside `bmad-review` were used instead, run as two parallel reviewers that only proposed edits. All proposals were applied to `brief.md` and `addendum.md`; none changes a decision. Main changes:
  - Terms are defined where first used: "warden" (the bosses), "the planning doc" (`docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`), milestones M0 to M6.
  - "Open" is replaced with "not decided / not chosen / not set yet".
  - The success measure moved to Version one; the Steam condition moved to "Not in version one".
  - The honest-edge differentiator moved to first place, and Risk 5 now has the Risk/Mitigation shape.
  - In the addendum: one source per row in the comparables table, Steam scores in one format, and "Price by length" moved under the table.
- **Length trim.** The figures in Market reality (Toki remake price, The Dark Queen of Mortholme, $347 median) were cut from the brief to a one-line summary. They remain in full in `addendum.md`. Reviewers flagged the brief at about 1,800 words against a 1 to 2 page target.
- **Status: final.** No required reviewers, external handoffs or on-complete steps are configured.
